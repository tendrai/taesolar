The Commercial Case

Businesses get the best returns from solar

Solar pays back fastest when you consume what you generate. Most businesses run their heaviest loads — air conditioning, refrigeration, pumps, machinery, lighting — during exactly the hours the sun is strongest.

That means little or no wasted export, and payback periods that are typically shorter than residential. Commercial systems also cost less per kilowatt as they scale.

We start with your load profile, not your roof. Before designing anything we look at how and when you actually consume power. Sizing to the roof rather than the load is the most expensive mistake in commercial solar.

Who We Work With

Built for how Phuket businesses actually operate

Different sectors have very different load shapes. We design around yours.

Hotels & resorts

Round-the-clock air conditioning, laundry and kitchen loads with large flat roofs — among the strongest solar candidates on the island.

Villas & rental estates

Pool pumps, staff accommodation and guest air conditioning running through the day across multiple metered buildings.

Factories & workshops

Steady weekday daytime demand from machinery and compressed air — close to ideal for near-total self-consumption.

Retail & restaurants

Refrigeration and cooling loads that track opening hours, where every unit generated offsets a unit you'd otherwise buy.

Common Questions

Commercial solar questions

How long does commercial solar take to pay back?

Typically around three to five years for a business with strong daytime consumption, though it depends heavily on your tariff class, load shape and how much of the generation you use directly. Businesses that operate seven days a week do better than weekday-only operations, because weekend generation isn't wasted.

Will installation disrupt our operations?

Rooftop work is largely independent of what happens below it. The disruptive part is the electrical tie-in, which requires a short shutdown — we schedule that for your quietest period, often overnight or during a maintenance window, and phase larger installations accordingly.

Can we export surplus power?

The 2026 household rooftop buyback scheme is for residential users up to 5 kW AC, so it doesn't apply to commercial systems. For businesses the economics come from self-consumption, which is why correct sizing matters so much — a commercial array that generates more than you use is capital you don't get back.

What about roof structure and warranties?

We assess structural loading before design and specify mounting appropriate to your roof type and its remaining life. If your roof is near the end of its life, we'll say so — installing a 25-year asset on a roof needing replacement in three years is a false economy.

Do you work outside Phuket?

Yes. We regularly work across Phang Nga and Krabi, and can deliver larger projects elsewhere in Thailand through our partner installer network while retaining project oversight.
